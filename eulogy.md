## Eulogy
### 1. The Empty Chair
It’s not an easy thing to lose a dad. No matter how old or strong you are, I don’t think anyone is ever prepared. 
For the past 13 days since you passed pa, I’ve dreamt about you, I’ll go through my day and suddenly a memory will come.
I remember when I was little, we would pray the rosary together with our family, even before I knew how to speak Indonesian. I would say “*Salam Maria, penuh rahmat…”*
I don’t even know what any of it meant, but that’s what you taught me. Faith.
Then another memory comes. The time we argued because I borrowed your car in Indonesia without your permission, or that night in Gold River when we went to in n out just the two of us, because neither of us could sleep. 
Dad, I’ve realized the human mind is a remarkable thing, memories of loved ones continue to exist even when that person isn’t on this earth anymore.
I’ve heard in some cultures, they say that the spirit lingers among us for 14 days before they depart.
So I took every opportunity I could to spend as much time with you, calling you (pa, i miss you, i love you), talking to you like you’re across from me at the dining table, we’ve shared moments of laughter, moments of tears, and conversations that lasted for hours.
And even now, I know you are standing beside me, cheering me on, giving your thumbs up, smiling through your tears because you’re so proud of me, your son, Ben. 
And now I’m beginning to understand what grief really feels like.
My beloved papa, I miss you so so much.
I miss when it was just Ben and Dad.

### 2. The Man You Were
*Do Small Things with Great Love*
These are words that you lived by. And of course, your famous 👍🏻 up gesture. 
Thank you to everyone that came today to honor my dad and everyone tuning in on Zoom.
Dad, you are unforgettable. I watched you stare death in the face, not once, not twice but three times. And each time you gave the grim reaper himself a thumbs-up from your hospital bed and survived. Doctors called you crazy, nurses called you a dead man walking, a medical anomaly, but to me you were just dad. 
As long as I have been alive, I have never met anyone with that kind of courage.
I once read that, to be recognized as a saint, a person must be credited with at least 2 miracles.
But in my eyes, dad, you’ve given me 6.
The first 3 were the heart attacks you survived, in 2018, 2024, and just a few weeks ago in 2026. Each time reminded us how resilient you were and gave us more time with you.
The other 3 miracles are the family you gave me: my dear mother, Janti, I love you, Mom, your eldest son and my older brother, Billy, and my number one favorite sister, Bella.
And then there’s me. (pause) The problem child.
The Catholic Church may have a few more steps before it would ever call you a saint. But in my heart, dad, you’ll always be St. Matthew the Brave.

I love you, Dad. And I know God cherishes you deeply.

### 3. The Man You Made Me Become

I thank God every day that I was born a Basuni. Many of you know I take great pride in that.
God blessed me with a father who gave me so many options and opportunities than I could ever count, simply by being Matheus’ son.
But, Pa, the greatest gift you ever gave me wasn’t being an American, or to acquire a trust fund. It wasn’t your assets. It wasn’t your Jaguars or your Range Rovers, although those are pretty nice.
The greatest gift you gave me was something far more valuable.
You gave me a reference model of what it means to be a man.
A man of unrelenting spirit. 
A man of courage. 
A man with stubbornness and fire to stand his ground.
But above all, a man whose life was rooted in faith, hope, and love.
You planted those seeds in me.
Pa, watching you tell the doctors that you would be willing to risk death on the way home, as you pulled me close and said “we are Basuni’s, we never give up, don’t listen to the haters” (jangan mau dengerin), it shook something in me, like a mini earthquake in my soul, and it touched and inspired me to my very core. 
In that moment, I felt your bravery, I felt the presence of the thousands of angels that had protected you throughout your life.
And when I look back now, looking through your photos, our family videos, I realize something.
At the end of it all, you were simply just a kid from Kalimantan doing his best.
And somehow… Look at what you built. Look at what you gave all of us. Look at what you gave me.

### 4. The Promise
I am so proud to call Matheus, oom mamat, bang mamat to his friends in Indonesia, Matt or Matthew to some of you, Mateo to others, my dad.
Not my friend. Not my uncle. My dad.
What an incredible privilege it has been.
Pa, you weren’t just someone people knew.
You were a friendly face to everyone whose lives you’ve touched. Your memory lives on in every person gathered here today, not because of the money you gave, or the titles you held, but because of how you made people feel.
Through your humor. Your wisdom. Your kindness. Simply by being yourself.
So today, let us remember him not only with our words, but with our lives. Let us continue the mission he showed us:
To be kind. To be a friendly face to strangers.
To remain humble, no matter our success or status. But above all…
To do small things with great love.

### 5. Until We Meet Again
Pa, I’m so proud of you. I’m proud of the man that you clawed your way to becoming, despite where you came from. 
You are forever a **legend in my eyes** and no one in my life comes close to what you have done for me and my family. 
I know you are standing beside me right now and will be for the rest of my life, giving me strength, courage, grit, discipline, perseverance and everything I need, to carry the Basuni Legacy forward.
I will continue to talk to you,
I will continue to pray with you, 
I will continue to do life with you, 
but most of all,
I just really, really miss you dad. 
Aku sayang banget sama kamu papa, sampai jumpa, safe travels, and I can’t wait to see you in heaven. 

Your son, Ben.
